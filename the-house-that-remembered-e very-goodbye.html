The House That Remembered Every Goodbye Chapter 1: The Key in the Envelope The envelope arrived three days after my mother's funeral. It was old, yellow at the edges, and addressed in handwriting I hadn't seen since childhood. Inside, I found a brass key, an electricity bill marked PAID, and a small piece of paper. There were only six words written on it: The house is waiting for you, Daniel. I didn't recognize the address. Ashcombe House, on a road that no longer appeared on any map I could find. I should have thrown the key away. Instead, I drove there the following Saturday. The road narrowed as I left the town behind. Trees crowded over the asphalt, their branches twisting together until the afternoon light disappeared. My phone lost its signal. The car radio dissolved into static. Then, around a bend, I saw the house. It stood on a hill behind a rusted iron gate, its tall windows dark and its roof sagging beneath years of rain. It looked abandoned, but something about it felt strangely familiar. I had never been there before. At least, that was what I believed. The brass key turned easily in the front door. Inside, the air smelled of damp wood, old books, and something faintly sweet—like the perfume my mother used to wear. Dust covered the furniture. A grandfather clock stood frozen at 4:17. Family photographs lined the hallway, but every face had faded except one. A little boy stood in the center of the photograph. He had my eyes. Before I could look closer, a bell rang somewhere upstairs. One clear, delicate note. Then another. I called out, asking if anyone was there. The house answered with the sound of a child's laughter. And from somewhere behind a locked door, a woman's voice whispered: "Daniel? You've taken so long." Chapter 2: The Room Upstairs I stood perfectly still. The woman's voice had sounded exactly like my mother's. Not similar. Not close. Exactly the same, down to the gentle crack in her voice whenever she said my name. But my mother was dead. I had watched them lower her coffin into the ground. "Who's there?" I called. No answer came. I climbed the stairs slowly, gripping the wooden railing. Each step creaked beneath my weight. At the top, a narrow corridor stretched into darkness. The bell rang again, this time from the very end. There were fourteen doors along the corridor. I counted them twice. The last door had a small brass number nailed to it: 14. Unlike the others, its paint was fresh. Someone had recently painted it a pale blue, the same color as the bedroom door in the house where I grew up. I tried the handle. Locked. A small pair of brown children's shoes sat neatly outside the door. They were covered in dust, except for their soles, which looked strangely clean. I bent to examine them. On the wall above the shoes hung a photograph. It showed a woman standing in front of Ashcombe House, holding a little boy's hand. Her face had faded almost completely. But I recognized the boy. He was the same child from the photograph downstairs. And beneath the picture, someone had scratched a date into the wood. October 17, 1998. My birthday. A sudden noise came from behind me. The grandfather clock downstairs had started ticking. I could hear every second, even from the upper floor. Then a small voice spoke through the locked door. "Daniel, did you bring her home?" I stepped backward. "Bring who home?" There was a long silence. Then the child whispered, "The woman who keeps saying goodbye." To Be Continued… What is hiding behind Room 14's locked door?
