<h1>The Customer Who Paid Tomorrow</h1>
<h2>An Original Psychological Horror Story</h2>

<p>At 2:13 every morning, someone bought the same three things from the supermarket where I worked.</p>
<p>A bottle of water.</p>
<p>A packet of table salt.</p>
<p>And a birthday cake.</p>
<p>Nothing unusual about that, except the customer never appeared on our security cameras.</p>
<p>The first time it happened, I blamed the old checkout system. The receipt printer had been malfunctioning for weeks, and management refused to replace it.</p>
<p>But the money was real.</p>
<p>The items were missing from the shelves.</p>
<p>And every receipt carried a date that hadn't arrived yet.</p>
<p>I worked the night shift at a supermarket outside Manchester. It was a boring job, mostly restocking shelves and listening to the refrigeration units hum. I'd taken it after dropping out of university, intending to stay for a month or two.</p>
<p>Eight months later, I was still there.</p>
<p>My supervisor, Martin, was the sort of man who considered every problem a personal insult. When I showed him the first receipt, he barely glanced at it.</p>
<p>“Computer glitch,” he said.</p>
<p>“It's dated tomorrow.”</p>
<p>“Then congratulations. You've discovered the future.”</p>
<p>He laughed at his own joke and walked away.</p>
<p>I put the receipt in my locker.</p>
<p>The following morning, I checked the stock.</p>
<p>One bottle of water was missing. One packet of salt was missing. One birthday cake was missing.</p>
<p>I stopped laughing about the glitch.</p>
<p>The next night, I watched the checkout monitor from the staff room.</p>
<p>At 2:13 a.m., the till beeped.</p>
<p>Three items appeared on the screen.</p>
<p>WATER — £0.85.</p>
<p>TABLE SALT — £0.60.</p>
<p>BIRTHDAY CAKE — £12.00.</p>
<p>The payment went through.</p>
<p>The receipt printed.</p>
<p>But nobody was standing at the checkout.</p>
<p>I rewound the camera footage.</p>
<p>At 2:12, the aisle was empty.</p>
<p>At 2:13, the till opened by itself.</p>
<p>At 2:14, it closed.</p>
<p>I watched the recording six more times.</p>
<p>Then I noticed something I hadn't seen before.</p>
<p>In the reflection of the glass freezer doors behind the checkout, a woman was standing directly behind me.</p>
<p>I spun around.</p>
<p>The staff room was empty.</p>
<p>On the monitor, the woman raised one hand and pointed at the screen.</p>
<p>At me.</p>
<p>I quit the next morning.</p>
<p>Or at least, I tried to.</p>
<p>Martin told me I needed to give a week's notice. I told him he could deduct it from my final pay. We argued for twenty minutes until he finally asked what had gotten into me.</p>
<p>I showed him the recording.</p>
<p>He watched it without speaking.</p>
<p>Then he closed the office door.</p>
<p>“Did you see her face?” he asked.</p>
<p>“No.”</p>
<p>“Good.”</p>
<p>He deleted the footage.</p>
<p>I stared at him.</p>
<p>“You know what this is?”</p>
<p>Martin rubbed his forehead.</p>
<p>“Three years ago, a woman died in this store. She was shopping for her daughter's birthday. There was an accident in the car park. The girl survived.”</p>
<p>I waited.</p>
<p>“After that, the night staff started finding strange receipts. Same three items. Same time. Every year, on the date of the accident.”</p>
<p>“Why didn't you tell me?”</p>
<p>“Because most people don't stay long enough to notice.”</p>
<p>He looked toward the office window.</p>
<p>“And because the girl is still alive.”</p>
<p>That evening, I went home and searched the local news archives.</p>
<p>I found the article in less than ten minutes.</p>
<p>A woman named Helen Ward had died outside the supermarket three years earlier. Her eight-year-old daughter, Sophie, had been taken to hospital but survived.</p>
<p>The article included a photograph of the mother and daughter.</p>
<p>The woman in the photograph was the woman from the freezer reflection.</p>
<p>I didn't return to work that night.</p>
<p>At 2:13 a.m., my phone rang.</p>
<p>It was Martin.</p>
<p>“Where are you?” he asked.</p>
<p>“Home.”</p>
<p>“Stay there.”</p>
<p>“What happened?”</p>
<p>He was breathing heavily.</p>
<p>“The till has printed another receipt.”</p>
<p>“Martin, I'm not coming back.”</p>
<p>“It's got your name on it.”</p>
<p>The call ended.</p>
<p>I sat on my sofa, staring at my phone.</p>
<p>Then a notification appeared from the supermarket's staff scheduling app.</p>
<p>My next shift had been changed.</p>
<p>Instead of 10 p.m. to 6 a.m., I was scheduled for 2:13 a.m. to 2:14 a.m.</p>
<p>The shift date was tomorrow.</p>
<p>I deleted the app.</p>
<p>I turned off my phone.</p>
<p>I locked the front door.</p>
<p>And I spent the rest of the night sitting with every light on.</p>
<p>At 2:13 the following morning, someone knocked.</p>
<p>Three times.</p>
<p>I didn't move.</p>
<p>A woman's voice came through the door.</p>
<p>“Excuse me? I think you have something that belongs to my daughter.”</p>
<p>I covered my mouth.</p>
<p>The voice was gentle. Tired. Almost apologetic.</p>
<p>“I've been trying to get it back for three years.”</p>
<p>Something slid beneath the door.</p>
<p>A supermarket receipt.</p>
<p>I waited until the footsteps disappeared before picking it up.</p>
<p>The receipt listed three items.</p>
<p>A bottle of water.</p>
<p>A packet of table salt.</p>
<p>A birthday cake.</p>
<p>Then, beneath the total, a fourth line appeared.</p>
<p>ONE HUMAN LIFE — PAID IN FULL.</p>
<p>Underneath it was my name.</p>
<p>And tomorrow's date.</p>
<p>I called the police.</p>
<p>They arrived, checked the hallway, and found nobody. One officer suggested it was a prank. The other asked whether I'd been sleeping properly.</p>
<p>I showed them the receipt.</p>
<p>They took it away.</p>
<p>By lunchtime, I'd convinced myself the whole thing was a cruel joke. I packed a bag and booked a train ticket to stay with a friend in another city.</p>
<p>I didn't care about my job.</p>
<p>I didn't care about my belongings.</p>
<p>I just wanted to be somewhere else when tomorrow arrived.</p>
<p>At the station, I bought a coffee and sat beside the departure board.</p>
<p>My train was delayed by fourteen minutes.</p>
<p>Then the announcement system crackled.</p>
<p>“Would passenger Daniel Price please report to platform three?”</p>
<p>My blood turned cold.</p>
<p>My name is Daniel Price.</p>
<p>I hadn't given it to anyone at the station.</p>
<p>I walked toward the platform office, hoping there was a rational explanation.</p>
<p>A station employee stood behind the glass window.</p>
<p>“Are you Daniel Price?”</p>
<p>“Yes.”</p>
<p>She handed me a small paper bag.</p>
<p>“Someone left this for you.”</p>
<p>Inside was a bottle of water, a packet of table salt, and a birthday cake.</p>
<p>On top of the cake was a single candle.</p>
<p>The label said SOPHIE — AGE 11.</p>
<p>I looked up at the employee.</p>
<p>“Who brought this?”</p>
<p>She frowned.</p>
<p>“You did.”</p>
<p>“No, I didn't.”</p>
<p>She turned her monitor toward me.</p>
<p>It showed a CCTV image of the station entrance, timestamped 2:13 that morning.</p>
<p>A man was walking through the doors carrying a paper bag.</p>
<p>The man had my face.</p>
<p>Same clothes.</p>
<p>Same haircut.</p>
<p>Same small scar above my eyebrow.</p>
<p>But he was smiling.</p>
<p>I ran.</p>
<p>I don't remember reaching the street. I only remember the sound of the station announcement following me into the rain.</p>
<p>“Passenger Daniel Price, your train is now boarding.”</p>
<p>I went straight to the police station.</p>
<p>The officer who had taken my receipt the day before recognized me immediately.</p>
<p>He brought me into a small interview room and closed the door.</p>
<p>“We found something,” he said.</p>
<p>He placed a photograph on the table.</p>
<p>It showed the car park outside the supermarket three years earlier.</p>
<p>A delivery van.</p>
<p>A damaged shopping trolley.</p>
<p>And a young man standing beside the trolley, looking directly at the camera.</p>
<p>My face.</p>
<p>I shook my head.</p>
<p>“That isn't me.”</p>
<p>The officer turned the photograph over.</p>
<p>On the back was a handwritten note.</p>
<p>The customer has paid. The replacement must arrive before midnight.</p>
<p>“Daniel,” the officer said carefully, “what happened three years ago?”</p>
<p>“Nothing. I was at university.”</p>
<p>He opened a folder.</p>
<p>Inside were copies of my university records, medical records, and birth certificate.</p>
<p>Then he showed me the final document.</p>
<p>A hospital report.</p>
<p>Three years ago, on the night Helen Ward died, a young man had been brought to the emergency department after a collision outside a supermarket.</p>
<p>He had no identification.</p>
<p>No wallet.</p>
<p>No phone.</p>
<p>His injuries were severe, and he died shortly after arrival.</p>
<p>The report included a photograph taken for identification.</p>
<p>The dead man's face was mine.</p>
<p>I stood up so quickly that the chair fell backward.</p>
<p>The officer told me to sit down.</p>
<p>I couldn't hear him.</p>
<p>I was looking at the date.</p>
<p>Three years ago.</p>
<p>The night before my first shift at the supermarket.</p>
<p>The night before I remembered anything about my life.</p>
<p>I walked out of the police station and kept walking until I reached the supermarket.</p>
<p>The doors were locked, but the lights were on.</p>
<p>Through the glass, I could see Martin standing behind the checkout.</p>
<p>He looked directly at me.</p>
<p>Then he unlocked the door.</p>
<p>“It's nearly midnight,” he said.</p>
<p>“What am I?”</p>
<p>Martin didn't answer.</p>
<p>He led me to the checkout and placed a birthday cake on the counter.</p>
<p>One candle.</p>
<p>Eleven years old.</p>
<p>“Sophie comes here every year,” he said. “Her father brings her on her birthday. They buy a cake, and she leaves flowers where her mother died.”</p>
<p>“Why am I involved?”</p>
<p>“Because Helen made a bargain.”</p>
<p>I laughed, though it sounded more like choking.</p>
<p>“With whom?”</p>
<p>Martin glanced at the ceiling.</p>
<p>“I don't know. I only know that she asked for one more year with her daughter. And every year, the shop takes someone who should never have been here in the first place.”</p>
<p>I looked at my hands.</p>
<p>They were trembling.</p>
<p>“So I'm already dead?”</p>
<p>Martin's silence answered me.</p>
<p>The checkout beeped.</p>
<p>A receipt began printing.</p>
<p>This time, the camera monitor showed the woman clearly.</p>
<p>Helen stood beside the till, holding the hand of a little girl.</p>
<p>Sophie.</p>
<p>The girl looked older than she had in the photographs. She was wearing a school uniform and carrying a small bunch of flowers.</p>
<p>Helen leaned down and whispered something to her.</p>
<p>Sophie looked toward the camera.</p>
<p>Then she shook her head.</p>
<p>“No,” she said.</p>
<p>Her voice came through the empty supermarket.</p>
<p>“Not him.”</p>
<p>The lights flickered.</p>
<p>Martin backed away from the till.</p>
<p>Helen stared at her daughter.</p>
<p>“You asked me to stay,” she said.</p>
<p>“I asked you to stay with me,” Sophie replied. “Not to take somebody else.”</p>
<p>For the first time, Helen looked frightened.</p>
<p>The receipt printer stopped.</p>
<p>The till opened.</p>
<p>Inside lay a small silver key.</p>
<p>I recognized it immediately.</p>
<p>It was the key to my apartment.</p>
<p>The place where I'd spent three years believing I was alive.</p>
<p>Sophie walked toward the checkout.</p>
<p>She placed her flowers beside the cake.</p>
<p>Then she looked straight at me.</p>
<p>“I'm sorry,” she said.</p>
<p>The lights went out.</p>
<p>When they came back on, the supermarket was empty.</p>
<p>Martin was gone.</p>
<p>The cake was gone.</p>
<p>The receipt was gone.</p>
<p>And for the first time in three years, I could remember dying.</p>
<p>I remembered the rain.</p>
<p>The headlights.</p>
<p>The sound of a little girl screaming.</p>
<p>I remembered Helen pushing her daughter out of the road.</p>
<p>And I remembered trying to reach them.</p>
<p>I had been the stranger who died that night.</p>
<p>The man nobody could identify.</p>
<p>The customer who paid tomorrow.</p>
<p>At sunrise, I walked out of the supermarket.</p>
<p>For the first time, the automatic doors didn't open for me.</p>
<p>I stood there until the sun rose fully over the car park.</p>
<p>Then I looked down.</p>
<p>My shadow was gone.</p>
<p>I never returned to the store.</p>
<p>But once a year, on Sophie's birthday, a receipt still prints at 2:13 a.m.</p>
<p>Three items.</p>
<p>One total.</p>
<p>And a fourth line that nobody can explain.</p>
<p>BALANCE REMAINING: ONE YEAR.</p>
